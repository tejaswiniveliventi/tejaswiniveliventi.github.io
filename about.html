ABOUT ME

A little bit about how I got here.

I've spent more than a decade working with enterprise software, Salesforce, data, and the people who use them. Somewhere along the way, I became just as interested in the questions behind the work as the technology itself.

WHERE IT STARTED

I started by supporting software.

My first job was as a support engineer for a reporting application written in ASP.

Support taught me something that has stayed with me: don't just fix the problem in front of you. Try to understand why it keeps happening.

I started asking questions like: Why are we doing this manually? Why is this data important? What are we actually trying to solve?

Those questions naturally led me toward automation, application modernization, and finding ways to monitor systems proactively instead of waiting for an alert to come knocking.

SALESFORCE & ENTERPRISE SOFTWARE

Then I found Salesforce.

I taught myself Salesforce and eventually spent much of my career building custom solutions and CRM applications for enterprise clients.

I've worked across Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, and enterprise Salesforce solutions, along with integrations, automation, SQL, and other enterprise technologies.

I've worked in roles spanning development, consulting, production support, and solution delivery. That range taught me to look at a system from more than one perspective.

I might be talking to a business stakeholder about a process in one meeting, a data team about reporting in another, and developers or architects about how to actually build it.

Being able to move between those conversations has become one of the things I value most about my experience.

MEASURING THE WORK

I became interested in the numbers behind the work.

I naturally started looking at operational metrics: How many features did we deliver? How many critical bugs made it into UAT? Did a particular fix actually reduce defects? Where were we spending time?

I wasn't looking at metrics just to produce a report. I wanted to understand whether the work we were doing was actually improving the way the team or business operated.

Over time, that curiosity about measurement and operational data grew into a much bigger interest in data itself.

DATA SCIENCE

So I went back to school.

I recently completed my Master's in Data Science.

My goal wasn't to leave everything I had done before behind. I wanted to add another way of looking at problems to the experience I already had.

My graduate studies gave me a stronger foundation in statistics, machine learning, SQL, Python, and data analysis, while my years in enterprise technology gave me the context to think about where those skills can actually be useful.

Now I'm exploring roles where I can bring those two sides together — particularly Salesforce and data or analytics roles where understanding both the systems and the business matters.

HOW I WORK

I tend to teach myself things.

A lot of what I've learned throughout my career has started with curiosity and a need to figure something out.

I'm self-motivated and a quick learner. When I don't know something, my first instinct is usually to learn enough about it to understand the problem and then figure out how to apply it.

I also like understanding the big picture. A technically correct solution isn't necessarily a good solution if it doesn't address the underlying business problem.

RIGHT NOW

I'm looking for what's next.

I'm interested in opportunities where I can use my Salesforce experience, software background, and growing data and analytics skills together.

I'm particularly drawn to work where there is a real problem to understand, data to make sense of, and a solution that can make someone's work a little easier.
